Dust motes and embers for comp, rebuilt

MoteField adds floating dust and fire embers to a shot in Nuke from the lighting department's utility pass, with no 3D particle render. It shows up in the Nuke viewer as an ordinary image, with depth of field and motion blur, to plus or over onto the comp. This page tracks the rebuild.

This update · 5 November 2026

  • The first frames. The new renderer draws the dust through the shot's own lens: depth of field with the header's blades and cat-eye, motion blur with the studio shutter, and holdouts weighted by coverage. About 1 second for a full HD frame (below).
  • Motion without a simulation. Each mote's path is worked out directly from the time, so any frame renders on its own: scrub anywhere, render on the farm in any order, no pre-roll. 0.8.3 needed 24 s to reach a frame mid-shot; v1 needs about 1.
  • The same look. Side by side with 0.8.3 on the same frame: the same beam and density, with total light within 3%.
  • Built for the Nuke viewer. No files to manage: the node keeps its own frame cache, named after its settings, so what you see is never stale. Alpha can be none (for plus) or a matte (for over). Moving things pushing the dust come last.

The new settings panel

Built from the settings table, as the Nuke node and the viewer will be. Four dials stay on screen, the tabs follow the order you work in, and each tab shows its basic settings until you ask for all of them. The tag on each setting says what changing it costs. Change something to see; double-click a setting's name to reset it.
