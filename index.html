THE PITCH

Dust that lives in the comp, driven by the shot camera and the lighting scene

Today dust motes are a 3D particle render. Every note on density, size, drift or focus means another render, and nobody sees the result until it comes back. MoteField moves the motes into Nuke as a node that reads the shot's camera, a P pass and the key light's depth map, then renders physically based motes in seconds. The viewer above is a working mockup of that node: the path-traced clay room, light and camera are stand-ins, but the optics and the light math are the real thing.

Why dust shows up in light shafts

  1. A mote is only visible when it scatters light toward the lens. Inside a shaft it is lit by the sun; in shadow it only sees weak bounce, so the shaft acts as a selector.
  2. Dust scatters forward. Looking back toward the light, a mote is roughly 10 to 30 times brighter than the same mote seen side-on. That is why shafts glow when the camera faces the window.
  3. Contrast. The lit air sits against shadowed walls, so even faint scattering reads.

How the node handles the light

  • Lighting renders one extra depth map per key light, from the light's point of view. It is the same data a shadow map uses and costs a few seconds.
  • Each mote is projected into that map: lit, unlit, or in the soft penumbra. Cookies, blinds and foliage come through for free.
  • Brightness uses a Henyey-Greenstein phase function with the real camera and light angles, so the shaft brightens as the camera turns toward the sun.
  • The same data drives an optional atmosphere pass, so god rays and motes always agree.

When there is no light data

  • Fall back to a screen-space light mask from the renderer's volume or diffuse AOV, sampled at each mote's projected position.
  • Or draw a light frustum in the Nuke 3D viewer: a spot or a window-shaped area light, with a card or roto as the cookie.
  • Either way the motes keep their true 3D positions, so defocus, motion blur and holdouts stay correct.
What dust is

Four kinds of mote, not one sphere

Household dust is mostly fine mineral grit, flakes of skin and paper, textile lint and small clumps of fluff. Each type has its own size range, shape, scattering and drag. Grit is a sub-pixel point of light at most distances. Flakes tumble, change brightness with their angle to the sun and throw brief glints. Lint is a curling strand, millimetres long, often dyed. Fluff is a loose, wispy clump. Shapes only resolve when a mote is large, close and in focus, exactly as in a real plate. Use the Loupe or the Close-up look to see them.
Defocus

Every mote renders its own bokeh

Circle of confusion comes from focal length, T-stop, focus distance and film back. Each mote's shape is spread through the lens aperture, with blade count, rotation and cat's eye toward the frame edge. A defocused lint fibre becomes a soft streak, not a disc. Energy is conserved, so an out-of-focus mote gets larger and dimmer, never brighter.
Motion blur

Shutter-accurate, from the mote and the camera

Each mote is projected at shutter open and shutter close using its velocity and the camera's matrices at both times, then swept between them. Glints are integrated across the shutter too, so they sparkle without strobing.
Holdouts

Per-pixel depth from the P pass

Motes behind geometry are held out using scene depth. Where the foreground is out of focus, a defocused coverage layer is used instead, so held-out edges match a ZDefocus'd plate rather than cutting a sharp line through soft geometry.
Embers

A second emitter for fire

The same node can emit embers from an area you place in the shot and size to the fire. Embers ride a buoyant plume that widens and slows with height, swirl in turbulence and wind, and cool as they rise, so their colour follows real black-body temperature from yellow-orange to dull red before they wink out. Pops of the wood throw short bursts of fast sparks. They get the same lens and shutter treatment as dust: streaks from motion blur, bokeh when out of focus, and depth holdouts. Try the Campfire scene.
Movement

Air, not noise

Curl-noise eddies at a chosen scale, slow drift, micro-turbulence, thermal lift inside the warm shaft and size-dependent settling: grit sinks, fluff and lint hang. A puff injects a real velocity field, for a door slam or a character walking through.
PHASE 1

BlinkScript prototype

GPU kernel in Nuke reading Camera3, the P pass and one light depth map. Outputs dust RGBA and mote depth. Enough to test on live shots.

PHASE 2

Deterministic sim cache

Seeded per shot and frame-addressable, so scrubbing, the farm and the artist's viewer all see the same motes. Motion vectors out for re-blur.

PHASE 3

NDK node + presets

C++ node with show presets (dusty attic, cathedral, exterior pollen), multi-light support and deep output for deep comps.

The mockup renders in WebGL in the browser. Click the viewer to rack focus, shift-click to puff air through the shaft, press L for a pixel loupe, and try the Looks along the top. Switch the Scene to Campfire for the ember tool. The ID channel colours each mote by type.
